In 1518, a woman in Strasbourg started dancing in the street... and she couldn't stop. Her name was Frau Troffea. She danced all day. Then all night. For almost a week. And then, other people joined her. Thirty. Then a hundred. Some accounts say up to four hundred people were dancing, for weeks, and nobody knew why. Their feet bled. They fainted from exhaustion. Some reports even say people died. So the city leaders asked the doctors for help. The doctors said it was hot blood... and the cure was more dancing. So the city built a stage, hired musicians, and even paid professional dancers to keep everyone moving. It did not work. It made it worse. Finally, the city banned music and dancing, and sent the dancers up into the mountains, to a shrine of Saint Vitus, where they were given red shoes and prayers. And slowly, the dancing stopped. To this day, nobody knows exactly why it happened. Historians think it may have been mass stress, after years of famine and disease. But one thing is certain. In 1518, a whole city danced... and couldn't stop.
