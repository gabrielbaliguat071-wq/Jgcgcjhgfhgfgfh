In 1925, a con man sold the Eiffel Tower. His name was Victor Lustig. One day, he read a newspaper story. The Eiffel Tower was rusting, painting it cost a fortune, and some people wondered if Paris should even keep it. Lustig had an idea. He printed fake government papers, and invited six scrap metal dealers to a fancy hotel. He told them, in secret: the city is selling the tower for scrap. Over seven thousand tons of iron. Highest bid wins. One dealer, Andre Poisson, wanted it badly. But his wife thought something was off. So Lustig did something genius. He asked Poisson for a bribe. Now Poisson was sure. Only a real government official would ask for a bribe. He paid for the tower, and he paid the bribe. Lustig took the money, and fled to Vienna. And Poisson? He was too embarrassed to tell the police. So a few months later, Lustig came back... and tried to sell the Eiffel Tower again. This time, the dealer went to the police. And Lustig escaped... again.
