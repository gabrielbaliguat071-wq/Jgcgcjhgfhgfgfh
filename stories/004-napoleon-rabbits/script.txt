In 1807, Napoleon had just signed a peace treaty, and he wanted to celebrate... with a rabbit hunt. His chief of staff, Alexandre Berthier, got everything ready. A big field. Lunch for the guests. And hundreds of rabbits, some say up to three thousand, waiting in cages. Napoleon arrived. The cages opened. The hunters raised their guns. But the rabbits did not run away. They ran straight at Napoleon. Hundreds of them, charging at the most powerful man in Europe. At first, everyone laughed. Then the rabbits started climbing up his legs. The coachmen grabbed their whips. The hunters waved sticks. Nothing worked. Napoleon ran back to his carriage, and the rabbits followed him. Some even jumped inside. Why? Berthier had not caught wild rabbits. He had bought tame rabbits from farmers. And tame rabbits think humans mean... food. At least, that is how one of Napoleon's generals told the story. So the man who conquered half of Europe... was chased off the field by bunnies.
