In 1932, Australia declared war... on birds. And lost. After World War One, thousands of soldiers were given land in Western Australia, to grow wheat. Then, about twenty thousand emus showed up. They ate the crops. They smashed the fences. And the holes let the rabbits in too. So the farmers went to the Minister of Defence, and asked for machine guns. The army sent Major Meredith, two soldiers, two machine guns, and ten thousand bullets. They even brought a cameraman, to film the victory. Day one. The emus saw them coming, and ran out of range. Day three. About a thousand emus walked right into an ambush. The soldiers opened fire... and the gun jammed. So they put a gun on a truck. But the emus were faster, and the ride was so bumpy, nobody could aim. Meredith said these birds could face machine guns with the invulnerability of tanks. After six days, the army went home. Then the emus came back. So the army came back too. In the end, they claimed nearly a thousand emus, with almost ten thousand bullets. That's about ten bullets per bird. Years later, the farmers asked for the army again. The answer was no. Instead, farmers got paid for every emu, and that worked better. And that's how the Australian army lost a war... to birds.
