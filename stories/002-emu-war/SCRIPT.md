# The Great Emu War (bean-head 2D short, own channel) — facts
- Nov–Dec 1932, Western Australia (Campion district); ex-WWI soldiers had been given farmland; ~20,000 emus moved into the wheat farms (commonly cited figure).
- Army sent Major G.P.W. Meredith + 2 soldiers (3 men), 2 Lewis machine guns, 10,000 rounds. Emus split into small groups; a gun mounted on a truck failed (truck too slow, ride too rough to aim).
- First week: only a small number of emus killed; troops withdrawn (8 Nov), returned later, then withdrawn again; emus kept coming.
- Meredith: "If we had a military division with the bullet-carrying capacity of these birds, it would face any army in the world."
Sources: Wikipedia "Emu War" (+ its cited contemporary newspapers), Australian National Museum / Smithsonian articles.

## v2 (longer, Ep. 1 of "Weird History") — extra facts
- Emus damaged fences, letting rabbits through (common account). Farmers' delegation went to Minister of Defence Sir George Pearce.
- A Fox Movietone cinematographer went along. 2 Nov 1932: emus out of range. 4 Nov: ambush at a dam, ~1,000 emus, a Lewis gun jammed after ~12 were shot.
- Meredith: "they can face machine guns with the invulnerability of tanks." Withdrawn 8 Nov; back 13 Nov after farmers' requests.
- Meredith's claim: 986 kills with 9,860 rounds (~10 rounds per bird). Farmers asked for military help again in 1934, 1943, 1948 — refused; a bounty system worked better.
